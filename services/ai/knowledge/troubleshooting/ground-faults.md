# Fire Alarm Ground-Fault Troubleshooting

## Safety

Follow site safety procedures, manufacturer instructions, project requirements, and authorized impairment procedures before disconnecting circuits.

Do not disable or impair a fire alarm system without proper authorization and notification.

## Common Causes

- Conductor contacting grounded metal
- Damaged insulation
- Moisture in a device or junction box
- Pinched cable
- Shield grounded at multiple locations
- Incorrect drain-wire termination
- Device contamination
- Damaged surge protection
- Field wiring connected to grounded equipment
- Water intrusion
- Incorrect power-supply interconnection

## Troubleshooting Workflow

1. Record the exact panel trouble message.
2. Identify all circuits associated with the reported fault.
3. Review recent work or environmental events.
4. Verify whether the fault is steady or intermittent.
5. Disconnect one field circuit at a time using an approved method.
6. Observe whether the ground-fault indication clears.
7. Divide the affected circuit into smaller sections.
8. Inspect junction boxes, devices, penetrations, and exterior locations.
9. Measure resistance to ground using an appropriate approved test method.
10. Correct the cause.
11. Restore all wiring.
12. Confirm all circuits are normal.
13. Perform required functional testing.
14. Document the repair.

## Intermittent Ground Faults

Check:

- Moisture
- Temperature changes
- Vibration
- Door movement
- Elevator travel
- HVAC equipment operation
- Cable movement
- Exterior devices
- Underground wiring
- Shared raceways
- Shield terminations

## Required Documentation

Record:

- Date and time
- Panel designation
- Trouble message
- Affected circuit
- Test points
- Measurements
- Corrective action
- Devices replaced
- Wiring repaired
- Final test results
- Technician
